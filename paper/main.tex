\begin{abstract}
Electricity options in emerging markets have to be valued without a traded
option surface and with a forward curve observed only as a strip of monthly
averages. A framework is developed that separates what such a market
identifies from what it does not. An hourly curve is built as the smoothest
one that reproduces the Turkish monthly baseload quotes exactly, and a
two-regime Ornstein--Uhlenbeck residual with transition probabilities driven
by residual demand is attached in centred form, so that the expected price
equals the forward for every parameter vector and under changes of measure in
the drift or the switching intensities. Single-hour options are priced through a
coupled moment system and finite differences. Independent
re-estimation shows that the inherited mean-reversion speed and volatility are
individually misidentified but reproduce the stationary dispersion observed in
2025, so values at 24 to 72 hours are insensitive to the former. Out of sample
in 2026, the forward curve exceeds realised prices by 385 to
794\,TRY/MWh on average and carries all of the bias, while the dispersion
assumed proportional to the price level is too narrow by up to a factor of
1.55. The unquoted front month bounds the precision of any reported value.
\end{abstract}
