\section{Residual dynamics and the centring identity}
\label{sec:residual}

\subsection{Transform and regimes}

Prices are bounded below by an administrative floor, can be zero or negative in
other markets \citep{AustHorsch2020}, and are heavy-tailed above. The
estimation underlying the production parameters works with the area hyperbolic
sine \citep{Schneider2011,Uniejewski2018},
\begin{equation}
  y_t=\operatorname{asinh}\!\left(P_t/s_P\right),\qquad P_t=s_P\sinh(y_t),
  \label{eq:asinh}
\end{equation}
with scale $s_P=282.48$\,\TRY. A continuous-time Markov chain
$S_t\in\{0,1\}$ distinguishes a calm regime $0$ and a stress regime $1$ with
transform-scale volatilities $\sigma^y_0=0.003535$ and
$\sigma^y_1=0.092407$ per $\sqrt{\text{hour}}$. The labels refer to variance
states of the residual, not to economic stress.

\subsection{Time-varying transition probabilities}

Hourly transition probabilities depend on standardised residual demand at a
one-hour lag,
\begin{equation}
  p_{01}(t)=\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_{01}+\gamma_{01}z_{t-1}\right),\qquad
  p_{10}(t)=\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_{10}+\gamma_{10}z_{t-1}\right),
  \label{eq:tvtp}
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})$, following \citet{Diebold1994} and \citet{Filardo1994}. Consistency of maximum
likelihood for this class is established by \citet{Pouzo2022}, and a
score-driven alternative is given by \citet{Bazzi2017}. Because $z_{t-1}$ is
observed before the transition it governs, the specification is predictive.
Over most of the covariate range the calm-to-stress probability exceeds the
reverse one, and the two cross near $z=1.4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}a). The
one-step matrix is mapped to a generator by the matrix logarithm,
\begin{equation}
  Q(t)=\begin{bmatrix}-q_{01}&q_{01}\\ q_{10}&-q_{10}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  q_{ij}(t)=\frac{\lambda(t)\,p_{ij}(t)}{s(t)},\qquad
  \lambda(t)=-\ln\!\left(1-s(t)\right),
  \label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
with $s=p_{01}+p_{10}$; the embedding is exact whenever $s<1$, which holds
throughout the sample. The regime-label reconciliation required by the
conversion is described in \ref{app:transitions}.

\subsection{Residual process}

Conditional on the regime, the residual follows an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck
process in price space,
\begin{equation}
  \dif X_t=\left[\kappa\left(m_{S_t}-X_t\right)+a_{S_t}\right]\dif t
           +\sigma^X_{S_t}(t)\,\dif W^{\Q}_t,
  \label{eq:ou}
\end{equation}
with a common mean-reversion rate $\kappa$, regime means $m_i$ and a drift
shift $a_i$ that is zero except where a risk premium is studied
(Section~\ref{sec:premia}). The diffusion is mapped from the transform scale
by the delta method evaluated at the forward level,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^X_i(t)=\sigma^y_i\sqrt{F(t)^2+s_P^2},
  \label{eq:sigmamap}
\end{equation}
so that price-scale dispersion is proportional to the price level whenever
$F\gg s_P$. This assumption is tested in Section~\ref{sec:variance}. The
process is additive and can in principle produce negative prices, and a shared
$\kappa$ lets regimes enter the second moment but not the speed of reversion,
which keeps the moment system linear. With the production parameters the
residual standard deviation saturates within about a day
(Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}b).

\subsection{Forward centring}

The spot price is defined as
\begin{equation}
  P_t = F(t) + X_t - \mu_X(t),\qquad \mu_X(t)=\E^{\Q}[X_t].
  \label{eq:centering}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[First-moment anchoring]
\label{prop:centering}
Let $X$ follow \eqref{eq:ou} with the regime chain generated by
\eqref{eq:generator}, let $\mu_X(t)=\E^{\Q}[X_t]$ be computed under the same
measure and parameters, and let $P$ be defined by \eqref{eq:centering}. Then
$\E^{\Q}[P_t]=F(t)$ for all $t$ and for every admissible parameter vector
\[
  \left(\kappa,\sigma^y_0,\sigma^y_1,m_0,m_1,a_0,a_1,\alpha_{01},\alpha_{10},
  \gamma_{01},\gamma_{10},\pi_0\right),
\]
including any change of the transition intensities of the form
$q_{ij}^{\Q}=q_{ij}e^{\eta_{ij}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By linearity, $\E^{\Q}[P_t]=F(t)+\E^{\Q}[X_t]-\mu_X(t)=F(t)$. The substance
lies in the requirement that $\mu_X$ be the same functional of the parameters
that drives the dynamics. Section~\ref{sec:moments} gives
$\mu_X=u_0+u_1$, where $u_i$ solve the moment system
\eqref{eq:momentsys}, which is integrated jointly with the regime
probabilities used in pricing; the cancellation therefore holds to integration
tolerance for any generator, including a transformed one.
\end{proof}

The identity confines every error in the residual layer to the shape of the
distribution around a forward level that stays fixed by the quotes. An earlier
multiplicative specification, $P_t=s_P\sinh(Y_t)$ with $Y$ a regime-switching
process, lacks this property: with the same parameters its implied expected
spot converges to about $-3{,}108$\,\TRY{} against a forward of
$+2{,}916$\,\TRY, because the regime-weighted mean of $Y$ is negative. Forward
consistency is either imposed or not obtained.
