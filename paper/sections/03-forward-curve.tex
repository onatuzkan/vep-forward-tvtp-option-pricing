\section{The hourly forward curve}
\label{sec:curve}

The framework is organised so that market information and historical
estimates enter through separate paths that meet only at the centring step
(Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). This section describes the market path.

\subsection{Averaging constraint and construction}

Let $\mathcal{H}_m$ be the delivery hours of month $m$ and $\mathrm{VEP}_m$
its quote. Settlement against the monthly mean requires
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_m|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}_m} F(h) = \mathrm{VEP}_m,
  \qquad m\in\mathcal{M},
  \label{eq:monthly}
\end{equation}
which fixes six linear functionals of a curve with $N=5{,}089$ hourly values.
The remaining degrees of freedom are chosen by a smoothness criterion, which is
an assumption and is reported as one. Following
\citet{FletenLemming2003} and \citet{BenthKoekebakkerOllmar2007}, the curve
$\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ solves
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf{F}}\;
  w_R\lVert D_2\mathbf{F}\rVert_2^2 + w_A\lVert\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{B}\rVert_2^2
  \quad\text{subject to}\quad A\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{b},
  \label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
where $D_2$ is the second-difference operator, $\mathbf{B}$ a baseline equal to
$\mathrm{VEP}_m$ within quoted months and to the anchor rule of
Section~\ref{sec:anchor} elsewhere, and each row of $A$ is one instance of
\eqref{eq:monthly} or the anchor condition. The problem is solved as one sparse
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker system,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    2\left(w_R D_2^{\top}D_2 + w_A I\right) & A^{\top}\\
    A & 0
  \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{F}\\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}\end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}2w_A\mathbf{B}\\ \mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
Because the quotes enter as equality constraints, the weights shape the curve
within months but cannot trade fit against smoothness. The monthly
reproduction error, $3.6\times10^{-12}$\,\TRY, is therefore a property of the
solver rather than evidence for the curve. Hourly increments average
$0.45$\,\TRY{} in absolute value, and the three largest lie at month boundaries
where the strip itself steps, the largest being $+47.0$\,\TRY{} between June
and July (Figure~\ref{fig:curve}a).

\subsection{The unquoted front month}
\label{sec:anchor}

The front month is never quoted. At each of the seven valuation dates examined
in Section~\ref{sec:strip}, from December 2022 to December 2025, the contract for
the next delivery month had already been delisted, so a short-dated option
always falls in a window without a forward price. The level there has to be
assumed. Three rules were compared: holding the spot flat, holding the next
quote flat, and interpolating linearly from the spot at the last known hour to
the first quote at the month boundary. The linear rule is used because it
satisfies $F(t_0)=P_{t_0}$, joins the February quote with a step of
$0.05$\,\TRY{} against 12 to 47\,\TRY{} at quoted boundaries, and does not
assert that January is a high-price month. Holding the February quote flat
violates the spot condition by 17\,\TRY. How much the choice matters is shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:curve}b and Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}: a shift of
$\pm20\%$ in the anchored level moves the 72-hour call at $K=3{,}000$ from
17.80 to 528.96\,\TRY.
