\section{Transition reconstruction and label reconciliation}
\label{app:transitions}

The original estimation output orders regimes so that state $0$ carries the
higher volatility, occupies about 67\% of the sample and has a mean duration of
$7.56$ hours, while state $1$ has a mean duration of $3.76$ hours. The
convention here is the reverse. Adopting it requires swapping the volatilities
and, because the logistic coefficients are indexed by the same labels,
swapping the transition coefficients crosswise, so that the production
$\gamma_{01}$ is the original $\gamma_{10}$ and conversely. Applying the swap
to only one of the two objects would leave volatilities and transitions
referring to different states without any single diagnostic revealing it; the
ordering $\sigma^y_0<\sigma^y_1$ is enforced when the parameters are loaded.

The output supplies slopes and mean durations but not intercepts. The
intercepts solve
\begin{equation}
  \E_z\!\left[\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_{01}+\gamma_{01}z\right)\right]
     = \frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{calm}}},\qquad
  \E_z\!\left[\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_{10}+\gamma_{10}z\right)\right]
     = \frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{stress}}},
\end{equation}
with the expectation taken over the full standardised covariate sample and
target rates of $0.2660$ and $0.1323$ per hour. The resulting pair reproduces
the reported stationary stress share of $0.6746$ to within 1.0\% under an
ergodic approximation and 4.5\% under simulation along the realised covariate
path. These values are moment-matching reconstructions without a sampling
distribution, which is why they are labelled as derived in
Table~\ref{tab:provenance}.
