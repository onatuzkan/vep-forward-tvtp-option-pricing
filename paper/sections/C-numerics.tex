\section{Numerical implementation and convergence}
\label{app:numerics}

The moment system \eqref{eq:momentsys} is advanced by fourth-order
Runge--Kutta with sub-steps of at most $0.25$ hours and coefficients
interpolated linearly within each step; on hourly steps without sub-stepping
the second-moment trajectory was unstable at the estimated switching
intensities. The pricing equation \eqref{eq:pde} is solved by Crank--Nicolson
on a uniform grid in the residual coordinate whose half-width is six terminal
standard deviations, with two time steps per hour and the regime coupling
treated in the same implicit step as the diffusion.

Table~\ref{tab:convergence} reports spatial convergence. The observed order is
$1.2$ to $1.7$ rather than the second order of Crank--Nicolson for smooth data,
which is the expected consequence of the kink in the payoff
\citep{Rannacher1984,GilesCarter2006}. Richardson extrapolation from the two
finest grids gives $166.686$\,\TRY, so the production grid of $1{,}201$ nodes
carries an error of $0.06$\,\TRY, or 0.04\%. Refining the time step eightfold at
$2{,}401$ nodes changes the value by $0.011$\,\TRY, below the spatial error,
and varying the domain half-width between four and nine standard deviations
changes it by at most 0.024\%. The discretisation error is therefore two orders
of magnitude below the effects reported in Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}.
Put--call parity, the centring identity under asymmetric premium
perturbations, and agreement with Monte Carlo simulation are run as automated
regression tests.
