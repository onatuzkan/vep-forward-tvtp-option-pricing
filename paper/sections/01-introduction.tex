\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The value of an electricity option is the expectation of its discounted payoff
under a pricing measure. Where options are traded, that measure is recovered
from the market: the forward curve fixes the drift and implied volatilities fix
the dispersion. Most emerging power markets supply neither in full, and the
Turkish market is a representative case. Its organised derivative segment, the
\emph{Vadeli Elektrik Piyasası} (VEP) operated by EPİAŞ, lists monthly baseload
futures, while options on power are not traded. A call written on the
day-ahead price of a given delivery hour therefore has to be valued with two
gaps: the forward curve is observed only as a strip of monthly averages, and
the distribution around that curve is not observed at all.

Each gap is addressed by a mature literature. On the distributional side,
regime switching is the established description of power prices. Following
\citet{Hamilton1989}, spikes have been modelled as visits to a separate state
rather than as jumps added to a single diffusion \citep{Deng2000,Huisman2003},
and heavy-tailed regime specifications have been found to fit spot data better
than single-regime alternatives \citep{Weron2009,JanczuraWeron2010}.
Jump-diffusion and threshold models \citep{CarteaFigueroa2005,GemanRoncoroni2006}
and their empirical comparison \citep{BenthKieselNazarova2012} complete the
picture, which is surveyed by \citet{Weron2014} and \citet{Deschatre2021}. Once
transition probabilities are allowed to depend on observed variables
\citep{Diebold1994,Filardo1994}, load, reserve margin and intra-day conditions
are found to carry information on switching
\citep{Mount2006,KanamuraOhashi2008,KarakatsaniBunn2008}. Residual demand, load
net of non-dispatchable renewable output, is the natural covariate
\citep{Wagner2014,Do2021}, and the merit-order literature explains the
mechanism \citep{Sensfuss2008,Ketterer2014,Clo2015}. For Türkiye the
merit-order effect has been measured by \citet{SirinYilmaz2020}, and the
forecasting properties of spot prices have been documented by
\citet{Ugurlu2018} and \citet{Ertugrul2022}. Because prices at and below zero
have become common, the area hyperbolic sine has largely replaced the
logarithm as the transformation of choice \citep{Schneider2011,Uniejewski2018}.

On the curve side, the decomposition of a power price into a deterministic
seasonal level and a mean-reverting residual \citep{LuciaSchwartz2002}, together
with its two-factor commodity counterpart \citep{SchwartzSmith2000}, underlies
most subsequent work. Building a granular curve from contracts that deliver
over a period is posed as a smoothing problem constrained to reproduce the
quoted averages \citep{FletenLemming2003,BenthKoekebakkerOllmar2007}, and
hourly versions have been constructed for European markets
\citep{Caldana2017,Kiesel2019}. Forward dynamics have been modelled
semi-parametrically \citep{Borak2008} and with factor models that respect
delivery-period averaging \citep{KieselSchindlmayrBorger2009,BenthBenthKoekebakker2008}.

Valuation on a regime-switching state is equally well developed. The
Markov-modulated framework goes back to \citet{Naik1993} and was extended by
\citet{BuffingtonElliott2002} and \citet{JobertRogers2006}, while changes of
measure acting on the switching intensities are treated by
\citet{ElliottChanSiu2005}. Finite-difference schemes for the coupled systems
are given by \citet{BoyleDraviam2007} and \citet{Huang2011}, applied to gas
storage by \citet{ChenForsyth2010}, and compared with Monte Carlo by
\citet{Zeng2017}. For electricity, derivatives are priced within a
regime-switching model through explicit risk premia by \citet{Janczura2014},
and spot and forward information are combined under regime shifts by
\citet{Paraschiv2015}, the closest antecedent to the present study.

What remains open is a question of identification. When a market supplies a
forward strip and nothing else, it has to be established which parameters of
such a model are determined by data and which are assumptions brought in by
the analyst. The distinction is easily obscured. A model that reproduces the
strip to numerical precision appears calibrated, yet volatilities and
switching parameters estimated under the physical measure $\PP$ and used under
$\Q$ without adjustment carry the assumption that no premium is charged for
volatility or regime risk. Forward premia in power markets are documented
empirically \citep{LongstaffWang2004,Viehmann2011,WeronZator2014,Algieri2021},
explained in equilibrium \citep{BessembinderLemmon2002,BenthCarteaKiesel2008}
and linked to systematic forecast errors \citep{Redl2009}. A premium that has
not been estimated is not thereby equal to zero.

\paragraph{Contribution.}
A valuation framework is built here around that separation, and four
contributions follow from it.

First, a forward-centred representation is proposed in which the spot price is
written as $P_t = F(t) + X_t - \E^{\Q}[X_t]$, with $F$ an equality-constrained
hourly forward curve and $X$ a two-regime Ornstein--Uhlenbeck residual with
time-varying transition probabilities. Subtracting the residual's own mean
makes $\E^{\Q}[P_t]=F(t)$ an identity for every admissible parameter vector
(Proposition~\ref{prop:centering}). The identity is a design property rather
than a deep result, but it insulates the one moment identified by the market
from those that are not, and it continues to hold under the two risk-premium
channels implemented here, a shift in the residual drift and a change in the
switching intensities.

Second, the inherited residual parameters are tested rather than assumed. The
residual process is re-estimated by maximum likelihood on a series constructed
to match the model, net of the calendar-month level and the hour-of-week shape.
The mean-reversion speed and the innovation scale of the production parameters
each differ markedly from these estimates, yet the two discrepancies offset:
the implied stationary standard deviation of the residual is 582.5\,\TRY{} at
the valuation forward, against 531 to 624\,\TRY{} realised in 2025. Because
option values at the reported maturities of 24 to 72 hours are governed by that
variance, the 72-hour call at a strike of 3\,000\,\TRY{} remains between 169 and
176\,\TRY{} across the estimated range of mean-reversion speeds when the
variance is held fixed, whereas values at maturities below about 12 hours are
not reliable. Time-varying transitions are strongly supported by
likelihood-ratio tests.

Third, the framework is evaluated out of sample. Under a valuation rule that
respects day-ahead publication times, predictive distributions are formed on 60
dates in 2026 with every parameter frozen at the end of 2025. The error is
dominated by the forward curve, which exceeds realised prices by 385 to
794\,\TRY{} on average across horizons, while the realised residual is 1.33 to
1.55 times wider than the model implies. When the variance comparison is
repeated at earlier dates, agreement holds from mid-2023 to the end of 2025,
with ratios of realised to implied dispersion between 0.93 and 1.13, and fails
in 2026. The failure is traced to the level-proportional scaling implied by a
fixed-scale transformation, which is identified as the main specification limit
of the model.

Fourth, the monthly strip is shown to bound what forward-anchored valuation can
deliver in this market. When the curve construction is replayed at seven
valuation dates from 2022 onwards, the December 2025 strip, above which
realised monthly prices fell by 1\,019\,\TRY{} on average, is found to lie
inside a distribution whose bias is large and unstable in sign. The daily
reference price of the monthly contracts changed on 1.0\% of contract-days for
2026 deliveries, so a delta hedge in the strip had no effectiveness over the
evaluation window. To the authors' knowledge, the predictive and hedging
performance of the Turkish monthly strip has not previously been measured.
