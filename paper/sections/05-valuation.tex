\section{Valuation}
\label{sec:valuation}

\subsection{Moment system}
\label{sec:moments}

The centring term and the terminal distribution both follow from
regime-partitioned moments,
\begin{equation}
  p_i(t)=\Q(S_t=i),\qquad
  u_i(t)=\E^{\Q}\!\left[X_t\mathbf{1}_{\{S_t=i\}}\right],\qquad
  w_i(t)=\E^{\Q}\!\left[X_t^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{S_t=i\}}\right],
\end{equation}
which satisfy the closed linear system
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \dot p_i &= -q_{ij}p_i + q_{ji}p_j,\\
  \dot u_i &= \kappa\left(m_i p_i - u_i\right) + a_i p_i
              - q_{ij}u_i + q_{ji}u_j,\\
  \dot w_i &= 2\kappa\left(m_i u_i - w_i\right) + 2a_i u_i
              + \left(\sigma^X_i\right)^2 p_i - q_{ij}w_i + q_{ji}w_j,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:momentsys}
\end{equation}
for $i\in\{0,1\}$ and $j\neq i$, started from $p_i(0)=\pi_i$ and
$u_i(0)=w_i(0)=0$, where $\pi$ is the filtered regime distribution at the last
known hour. The structure is recursive, $p$ being autonomous, $u$ depending on
$p$ and $w$ on both, so no closure approximation is needed. Then
$\mu_X=u_0+u_1$ and $\operatorname{Var}^{\Q}[X_t]=w_0+w_1-\mu_X^2$; the
derivation is given in \ref{app:moments}. Because $q_{ij}$ and
$\sigma^X_i$ vary along the covariate path, the system is non-autonomous and is
integrated with sub-hourly steps (\ref{app:numerics}).

\subsection{Pricing equation and verification}

Conditional on the regime, the value function $V_i(x,t)$ solves the coupled
system
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t V_i
  + \left[\kappa\left(m_i-x\right)+a_i\right]\partial_x V_i
  + \tfrac12\left(\sigma^X_i(t)\right)^2\partial_{xx}V_i
  - rV_i + q_{ij}(t)\left(V_j-V_i\right) = 0,
  \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
for $j\neq i$, with terminal condition $V_i(x,T)=\left(F(T)+x-\mu_X(T)-K\right)^{+}$, in the
form of \citet{BuffingtonElliott2002} and \citet{JobertRogers2006}. It is
discretised by Crank--Nicolson finite differences
\citep{BoyleDraviam2007,Huang2011} on $1{,}201$ nodes, and the option value is
the $\pi$-weighted mixture of $V_0(0,0)$ and $V_1(0,0)$. The moment system and
the pricing equation draw $a_i$ and $q_{ij}$ from one parameter field, since a
perturbation applied to only one of them breaks
Proposition~\ref{prop:centering} without any visible symptom in prices; a
regression test requires $\max_t|\E^{\Q}[P_t]-F(t)|<10^{-8}$\,\TRY{} under
asymmetric perturbations.

Put--call parity holds to $1.0\times10^{-6}$\,\TRY{} across the
strike--maturity grid. A direct simulation of \eqref{eq:ou} with exact
conditional Ornstein--Uhlenbeck steps on the sampled regime path gives, with
$5\times10^{5}$ paths, $166.11\pm0.40$\,\TRY{} for the 72-hour call at
$K=3{,}000$, against $166.71$\,\TRY{} from the finite-difference solution on
$2{,}401$ nodes, inside the 95\% interval. The production grid value is
$166.75$\,\TRY, and Richardson extrapolation places the discretisation error at
0.04\% (\ref{app:numerics}).

\subsection{Risk-premium channels}
\label{sec:premia}

The residual parameters are estimates under $\PP$, so using them for valuation
asserts that neither volatility nor regime risk is priced. The strip cannot
identify either premium, and both are implemented as explicit channels whose
default reproduces the zero-premium values exactly. The first is the drift
shift $a_i$ in \eqref{eq:ou}, in the spirit of \citet{Janczura2014}. It enters
the first moment through $a_ip_i$ and the second through $2a_iu_i$, so its
effect on the variance, and through it on option value, is of second order: a
log-log regression of the price change on $a_1$ has slope $2.0000$. The second
channel acts on the switching intensities,
\begin{equation}
  q^{\Q}_{ij}(t)=q_{ij}(t)\,e^{\eta_{ij}},
  \label{eq:q2}
\end{equation}
a change of measure of the type studied by \citet{ElliottChanSiu2005}. The
multiplicative form keeps the generator valid for every real $\eta$, and the
same transformed generator enters \eqref{eq:momentsys} and \eqref{eq:pde}, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:centering} continues to hold. Because $\eta$ moves the
stationary regime occupancy, its effect on the terminal variance is of first
order.

The size of either premium cannot be estimated without option prices. An
ex-post premium panel, built from seven VEP snapshots and realised prices up
to the valuation cutoff, has five or six observations per horizon, a pooled
mean of about $625$\,\TRY{} and block-bootstrap standard errors of 280 to
530\,\TRY. Translated through $|a|=\kappa L$, the pooled mean corresponds to
$|a|\approx 49$\,\TRY{} per hour, which is used below as an upper bound for the
drift channel. No comparable anchor exists for $\eta$, and the range
$|\eta_{ij}|\le0.5$ is a scenario rather than an estimate.
